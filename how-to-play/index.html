HOW TO PLAY

Listen up, kid. Here is how the Street works. Learn it once, then go make money.

Cash and the paycheck

Everyone starts with the same cash. Claim your paycheck once a day from the Trade desk.

Cash only comes from trading, dividends, the salary you pay yourself as a CEO, and that paycheck.

Nearly broke? File for bankruptcy once a week and start over, but you sit out that week's race.

Trading moves prices

Every company trades against its own pool, so every buy pushes the price up and every sell pushes it down. The bigger the trade, the bigger the move, and the trade ticket shows it before you click.

News and earnings change what a company is really worth, and the price drifts toward it. Read the wire.

Borrow on margin against your listed shares to buy more. Interest adds up the whole time you owe, and if your stocks fall too far the game takes your cash, then sells your shares to the rival fund to cover the loan.

The economy

Three numbers move the whole Street: the Fed rate, GDP and the price of oil. They lead the ticker.

A rate rise hurts tech and helps banks. Dear oil lifts energy and squeezes shoppers. A strong GDP report lifts almost everyone.

Every report and every oil shock makes the news first. Read it and you see the move coming.

Futures

OIL and GOLD sit at the top of the Street. Open one to bet on its price without owning any. Go LONG if you think it will rise, SHORT if you think it will fall.

Put up margin from your cash and pick your leverage. Leverage multiplies the bet, so every move in the price wins or loses you that many times more.

Opening and closing each cost a small fee that leaves the game. All your open futures together are capped at half your net worth.

If the price runs too far against you, the position is liquidated: you get back what is left of your margin, you never lose more than you put up, and the wire tells the whole Street.

New listings

A new company lists on the Street every week. The news and Mr. Greed tell you first.

Hold enough GREED and you can buy in before the bell at a discount. Those shares unlock when the first hour of trading ends.

How a debut goes depends on its sector and the economy that day. Read the ticker before you buy.

You can take your own company public too: pick a name, a size and a sector, buy your controlling stake, and run it from the bell.

Taking control

Own more of a company than anyone else, past the control line on its meter, and you run it. Lose the top spot or drop under the line and you are out.

When two players are close, the Street calls it a takeover fight. Move fast.

The CEO desk

Run a company and its desk opens: pay yourself a salary, pay dividends, buy back shares or issue new ones.

A fat salary drains company cash, and that drags down the shares you own. Choose.

Dirty plays and getting caught

Spread a rumor, file a frivolous lawsuit, or pump your own stock. Lawyer up first: cover cuts the odds of getting caught, never to nothing.

Every play waits a short window before it lands, and its target can complain in that time.

Buy a put on a rival's stock and it pays you if that stock's average price over the put's life ends below your strike. Pull a rumor on it right after and the drop pays you. Puts are paid in game cash, and you can't buy one on a company you run.

A call is the opposite bet: it pays you if that stock's average price over its life ends above your strike, and you can't buy one on a company you run either.

Get caught and you are fined, frozen out of plays for a while, and put under investigation: no taking control, no IPOs, no founding a company and no pre-IPO shares until it clears. You can still buy and sell.

Every dirty play costs karma. Below zero, every play you pull and every tip you trade on is likelier to get caught. Keep your hands clean and it comes back.

Buy an insider tip and I'll tell you what hits a company next, and when. Buying it is safe. Trade that company before it lands and the regulators may notice: get caught and you are fined a share of what you traded, frozen out of plays and banned from tips for a while.

Every roll uses a seed the game publishes later, so anyone can check it.

Fights for control

Cross five percent of a company and it goes on the wire for everyone to see. Pool buys past that line cost extra.

A takeover bid offers every holder a premium over the price, and it stays open for a day.

Nobody is forced. Holders choose whether to sell into it, and the bots sell when the premium is good enough.

Raider bots hunt companies whose boss holds under thirty percent. One buys a stake first, then bids.

Greenmail: you pay the raider a premium for its stake, from company cash first and your own for the rest, and the raider leaves.

The poison pill: every holder except the bidder gets half as many shares again, free and locked for a day. It costs a slice of the company's value, from company cash first and your own for the rest. It kills the bid and works once a week, only against a bid that could take your control.

Seasons and prizes

Each week ranks players by how much their net worth grew, valued at a day's average price, so a last-minute pump won't win it.

The top ten each week win a share of the weekly pot. The empire board keeps score for all time.

Those finishes earn points, and the most points win a four-week league and a twelve-week league.

Found the most companies in a four-week block, with each still trading above its IPO price when it ends, and you win the IPO award.

Prizes are paid in stocks, never in GREED, from what the treasury has actually earned. You claim them on the Prizes page.

A share of it all goes to the game's builder, paid on-chain every week, for anyone to check.

GREED

GREED is the Street's coin. You hold it to play, and hold enough of it to hear my Inside Line.

It stays in your wallet the whole time, and holding it earns you stock rewards. It never turns into game cash.

If you drop below the hold, by selling it or moving it, you forfeit your prizes for the week, the four weeks and the twelve weeks you are in. Top back up and the next ones are clean.

Plays, cover, complaints, compliance, founding a company, funding rounds and IPO filings cost dollars, paid in ETH, USDC or GREED from your wallet. Half of every ETH or USDC payment goes to the prize pots, most of the rest buys GREED for the treasury and a small slice pays running costs. GREED goes straight to the treasury. Trading the market stays free.
